Meeting recorders solve one hour of your day and forget the other seven. The
notes land in their own app, in their own silo, disconnected from the work the
meeting was actually about.

ShogunAI treats a meeting as what it is: a dense hour of the same day the rest
of the memory layer already covers.

## How it works

ShogunAI notices you are in a meeting — the call app is frontmost, the mic is
live, the event is on your calendar — and starts a transcript. No bot joins the
call. Nobody has to be told a recorder is present, because there is no
recorder. When the meeting ends you get a recap: what was decided, what was
assigned, what stayed open, and who owes what to whom.

Those outputs are not a separate document. Commitments made in the call become
open loops in the same tables that track the rest of your week, so the follow-up
you promised at 15:40 shows up in the morning brief and in the drafted email —
not in a notes app you would have had to remember to open.

## What is stored, and what is not

**Stored:** the transcript text, who said it, when, and which meeting it came
from.

**Not stored:** the audio. ShogunAI does not write a recording to disk, does
not keep a temporary audio file, and does not produce one at any point in the
pipeline. There is nothing to leak, nothing to accidentally share, nothing to
subpoena. This is enforced in the build itself, not by remembering to delete
things afterward.

Language handling matters here, so the transcription is multilingual by
default. A call that switches between Japanese and English comes back as one
transcript, not two broken halves.

## The part we will not bury

Live transcription runs through a speech service rather than entirely on your
Mac. Audio streams to it for the length of the call and is used only to produce
the text — the account is configured so that nothing spoken in your meetings
can be used to train or improve anyone's models.

That is a real exception to "your data stays on your machine," and it is the
reason we are writing it in plain language here instead of in a footnote. The
app discloses it in the meeting interface, the traceability log records it like
any other egress, and if you would rather not, meeting notes can stay off
without affecting anything else.

We picked this trade deliberately. On-device transcription that is fast enough
to be live, accurate enough to be quoted, and multilingual enough to be useful
in the meetings our users actually sit in is not there yet on the hardware
people actually own. When it is, this moves back to the Mac.

## Included in every plan

Meeting notes are in Standard, in Pro, and in the seven-day trial. This is a
feature you have to feel to evaluate, and a trial that hides it is a trial that
cannot answer the question you are trying to answer. The only meeting-related
feature behind Pro is reaching meeting memory from your own AI tools through
the Memory API.

Recording a meeting is easy. Having the meeting change what your system does
for the rest of the week is the part worth building.
