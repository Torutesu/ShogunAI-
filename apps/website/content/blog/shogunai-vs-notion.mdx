ShogunAI and Notion solve related but different problems. Notion is a flexible workspace for writing, organizing, and sharing information. ShogunAI is designed to make the context around your work easier to capture, retrieve, and act on.

## Quick comparison

| | ShogunAI | Notion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary role | AI memory layer for work context | Workspace for pages, databases, and wikis |
| Information model | Context captured across work | Information intentionally organized into pages |
| Best for | Recovering decisions and background quickly | Creating a shared source of truth |
| Search behavior | Natural-language recall across context | Search across workspace content |
| Privacy position | Local-first by default | Cloud workspace model |

## Choose Notion when

Notion is a strong choice when your team wants a collaborative workspace, structured documentation, project databases, or a wiki that people actively maintain.

## Choose ShogunAI when

ShogunAI is a better fit when important context is spread across conversations, email, documents, and decisions—and manually updating a knowledge base is not realistic.

## Can they work together?

Yes. A workspace and a memory layer can complement one another: Notion can hold the information your team deliberately publishes, while ShogunAI helps recover the broader context that led to those decisions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ShogunAI a replacement for Notion?

No. They have different centers of gravity. Notion is a workspace; ShogunAI is designed to help you remember and use work context.

### Which one is better for a knowledge worker?

It depends on where your information lives. If most work is curated into pages, Notion may be enough. If context is scattered across tools and conversations, ShogunAI addresses a different problem.
