ShogunAI and Glean both address the problem of scattered work knowledge, but they target different centers of gravity. Glean is known for enterprise knowledge discovery across company systems. ShogunAI is being built as a private memory layer for the individual knowledge worker.

## Quick comparison

| | ShogunAI | Glean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary role | Private AI memory for individual work | Enterprise knowledge search and discovery |
| Best for | Founders, builders, researchers, and operators | Organizations with many internal systems and teams |
| Context model | A person's ongoing work context | Company knowledge and governed sources |
| Main question | “What happened and what should I do next?” | “Where is the information our organization has?” |
| Deployment focus | Local-first, personal control | Enterprise administration and integrations |

## Choose Glean when

Glean is a strong candidate when an organization needs a governed search layer across many enterprise systems, with administrative controls and company-wide discovery.

## Choose ShogunAI when

ShogunAI is designed for people who need a private, persistent memory of their own working day and want to recover context without turning every decision into a formal knowledge-base entry.

## The important distinction

Enterprise search answers “where is the information?” A personal AI memory aims to answer “what did I decide, why did it matter, and what should happen next?” Those questions can overlap, but they are not identical.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ShogunAI an enterprise search replacement?

No. The products are oriented around different users and jobs. Glean focuses on organization-wide discovery; ShogunAI focuses on personal work context.

### Which tool is better for a small startup?

It depends on the problem. If the immediate need is company-wide search and governance, evaluate enterprise search. If the immediate need is helping each person remember and act on their own context, a personal memory layer may be a closer fit.
