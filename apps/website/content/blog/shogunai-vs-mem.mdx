ShogunAI and Mem both help people retrieve information later, but they start from different workflows. Mem is centered on capturing and recalling personal notes. ShogunAI is designed to make the context around your work searchable across the tools where that work happens.

## Quick comparison

| | ShogunAI | Mem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary role | AI memory layer for work context | AI-assisted personal notes |
| Starting point | Context across work tools | Notes and information you save |
| Best for | Recovering decisions, conversations, and next steps | Organizing personal knowledge and ideas |
| Workflow | Capture, recall, then act | Capture, organize, then recall |
| Privacy focus | Local-first product direction | Review current storage and privacy terms for your plan |

## Choose Mem when

Mem is a good fit when your main habit is writing down ideas, notes, and personal knowledge and you want AI help finding connections between those notes.

## Choose ShogunAI when

ShogunAI is a better fit when important context already exists in conversations, email, documents, and decisions, and you do not want to rely on remembering to write everything down.

## Can a notes app and a memory layer work together?

Yes. Notes are useful for the knowledge you intentionally curate. A memory layer helps recover the surrounding context: what was discussed, what changed, and why a decision was made.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ShogunAI a note-taking app?

No. It is designed around the context around your work, not only the notes you manually create.

### Which is better for researchers?

Researchers who primarily maintain a personal knowledge base may prefer a notes-first tool. Researchers who move between sources, conversations, and project decisions may benefit from a broader work-context memory.
